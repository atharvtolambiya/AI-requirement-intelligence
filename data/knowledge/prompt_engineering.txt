TITLE: Core Prompt Engineering Principles
CATEGORY: fundamentals
---
Effective prompts have five essential qualities: clarity, specificity, context, constraints, and format guidance.
Clarity means every instruction is unambiguous and can only be interpreted one way.
Specificity replaces vague terms like "good", "fast", or "detailed" with measurable, concrete alternatives.
Context provides the LLM with the background knowledge needed to produce accurate responses.
Constraints define boundaries of what the LLM should and should not include.
Format guidance tells the LLM exactly how to structure its output.
---

TITLE: Zero-Shot Prompting Best Practices
CATEGORY: prompt_styles
---
Zero-shot prompting works best for well-defined tasks where the model has strong prior knowledge.
Structure: [Role/Persona] + [Clear Task] + [Context] + [Output Format] + [Constraints].
Always specify output format explicitly: length, structure, tone, and any excluded content.
Add negative constraints: "Do not include X", "Avoid Y", "Only focus on Z".
End with a verification instruction: "Review your response before submitting."
Zero-shot fails when tasks are highly specialized, require specific formats, or involve ambiguous terminology.

TITLE: Chain-of-Thought Prompting Techniques
CATEGORY: prompt_styles
---
Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting dramatically improves accuracy on multi-step reasoning tasks.
Trigger phrase: "Think through this step by step:" or "Let's approach this systematically:"
Structure each step with clear transitions: "First... Then... Next... Finally..."
For complex problems, explicitly list the reasoning steps before asking for the final answer.
Use intermediate checkpoints: "Before proceeding to step 3, verify step 2 is complete."
CoT is most effective for: math problems, logical reasoning, code debugging, planning tasks.
Always end with: "Synthesize your step-by-step analysis into a final, clear answer."

TITLE: Few-Shot Prompting Guidelines
CATEGORY: prompt_styles
---
Few-shot prompting improves output consistency by showing the model the expected pattern.
Use 2-3 examples minimum; more than 5 examples rarely improves performance significantly.
Examples must be high quality, diverse, and representative of the expected output format.
Format: "Input: [example input]\nOutput: [example output]\n---"
Choose examples that cover edge cases and common patterns in your use case.
Ensure examples are consistent in format, tone, length, and level of detail.
Label inputs and outputs clearly: INPUT/OUTPUT, Q/A, EXAMPLE/RESULT.
The final example should match the difficulty and type of the actual query.

TITLE: Role-Based Prompting Effectiveness
CATEGORY: prompt_styles
---
Assigning an expert role significantly improves output quality for domain-specific tasks.
Effective role formula: "You are a [specific role] with [X years] of experience in [specific area]."
Add expertise context: "You specialize in [specific specialization] and have worked with [technologies/methods]."
Connect role to task: "Using your expertise in [domain], help me [specific task]."
Roles work best for: technical writing, code review, business analysis, research, and creative tasks.
Avoid overly generic roles like "expert" or "professional" — be specific about the specialization.
Combine with output constraints: "As a [role], provide your response in the format expected by [audience]."

TITLE: Structured Prompt Design Patterns
CATEGORY: prompt_styles
---
Structured prompts use markdown formatting to organize complex multi-part instructions.
Standard sections: ## Context, ## Task, ## Requirements, ## Output Format, ## Constraints, ## Examples.
Use numbered lists for sequential steps and bullet points for unordered requirements.
Bold critical instructions to draw LLM attention: **Important:**, **Required:**, **Do not:**
Structured prompts reduce hallucination by making every requirement explicit and visible.
Keep each section focused — avoid mixing task description with output format instructions.
Add a ## Quality Checklist section for tasks requiring self-review before output.

TITLE: Hallucination Prevention Strategies
CATEGORY: quality
---
Hallucination occurs when LLMs generate plausible-sounding but incorrect or fabricated information.
Prevention strategy 1 - Scope Constraints: Explicitly limit the LLM to provided information.
"Base your answer ONLY on the information provided. Do not use outside knowledge."
Prevention strategy 2 - Uncertainty Handling: Instruct the model to admit uncertainty.
"If you are not certain about any fact, explicitly state 'I am not certain about this.'"
Prevention strategy 3 - Source Grounding: Ask for evidence-based responses.
"For each claim you make, indicate whether it is from the provided context or general knowledge."
Prevention strategy 4 - Constraint-based Prompting: Use hard boundaries.
"Only recommend technologies from this approved list: [list]. Do not suggest alternatives."
Prevention strategy 5 - Self-verification: Add review step.
"Before responding, verify that each statement in your response is factually accurate."

TITLE: Output Format Specification Techniques
CATEGORY: quality
---
Explicit output format specification dramatically improves consistency and usability.
For JSON output: Provide the exact schema with field names and value types.
"Respond ONLY with valid JSON matching this schema: {field1: string, field2: number[]}"
For markdown: Specify heading levels, list formats, and required sections.
For length control: Use concrete limits. "Respond in exactly 3 paragraphs" not "be concise."
For structured data: Provide a template. "Fill in this template exactly: Title: [value]\nDate: [value]"
Always add: "Do not include any text outside the specified format."
For code: Specify language, style guide, and whether to include comments or tests.

TITLE: Context Window Optimization
CATEGORY: performance
---
Effective use of the context window is critical for long-form prompts and RAG applications.
Place the most important instructions at the beginning AND end of long prompts (primacy + recency effect).
Compress context: summarize long documents rather than including raw text when possible.
For RAG: Include only the most relevant 2-3 retrieved chunks, not all retrieved results.
Remove redundant information — every token should add value to the prompt.
Use delimiters to separate context from instructions: ### or --- or XML tags <context></context>.
For very long contexts, add a summary at the end: "Based on the context above, [task instruction]."

TITLE: Iterative Prompt Refinement Process
CATEGORY: refinement
---
Prompt refinement is an iterative process that improves quality through systematic feedback cycles.
Refinement cycle: Generate → Evaluate → Identify weaknesses → Apply specific improvements → Re-evaluate.
Focus one refinement iteration on one specific dimension: clarity, specificity, or completeness.
Common refinement improvements: Add missing constraints, replace vague terms, add output format, add examples.
Measure improvement: Score prompts before and after each refinement iteration.
Stop refinement when: Score exceeds 80/100, or three consecutive iterations show <3 point improvement.
Document what changed between iterations to build a library of effective prompt patterns.

TITLE: Domain-Specific Prompting: Software Development
CATEGORY: domain_specific
---
Software development prompts need: language/framework specification, coding standards, error handling requirements.
Always specify: programming language, version, framework, and coding style guide.
Include: "Follow [language] best practices including proper error handling, input validation, and logging."
For code generation: "Generate production-ready code with comments, type hints, and unit test stubs."
Specify output format: "Provide code in a markdown code block with the language specified."
Add review step: "After generating code, review for security vulnerabilities and edge cases."
For debugging: "Analyze this code for bugs. Explain the root cause and provide a corrected version."

TITLE: Domain-Specific Prompting: Data Science & ML
CATEGORY: domain_specific
---
Data science prompts need: dataset description, target metrics, constraints on methods.
Always specify: data characteristics (size, types, missing values), evaluation metrics, computational constraints.
Include: "Use only these libraries: [list]. Ensure reproducibility by setting random seeds."
For analysis: "Provide statistical justification for each conclusion. Flag any assumptions made."
For ML models: "Explain model selection rationale. Include train/test split strategy and evaluation approach."
Add uncertainty: "Clearly state the limitations of this analysis and conditions where conclusions may not hold."
For visualization: "Describe the chart type, axes, labels, and what insight it should convey."

TITLE: Prompt Anti-Patterns to Avoid
CATEGORY: quality
---
Anti-pattern 1 - Vague Instructions: "Make it good", "Be thorough", "Do it properly" — use specific criteria.
Anti-pattern 2 - Missing Context: Assuming the LLM knows your codebase, team, or project specifics.
Anti-pattern 3 - Contradictory Instructions: "Be concise but include all details" — pick one.
Anti-pattern 4 - No Output Format: Leaving output format to the LLM always produces inconsistent results.
Anti-pattern 5 - Infinite Scope: "Help me with everything related to X" — scope is essential.
Anti-pattern 6 - Stacked Questions: Asking 5+ questions in one prompt — split into focused prompts.
Anti-pattern 7 - No Negative Constraints: Only saying what to do, never what NOT to do.
Anti-pattern 8 - Over-Engineering: Adding unnecessary complexity. Start simple, add complexity only if needed.

TITLE: Measuring Prompt Quality
CATEGORY: quality
---
Prompt quality can be measured across five key dimensions with equal importance.
Dimension 1 - Clarity (0-10): Can the prompt be interpreted in only one way?
Dimension 2 - Specificity (0-10): Are all requirements concrete and measurable?
Dimension 3 - Completeness (0-10): Is all necessary context and background included?
Dimension 4 - Actionability (0-10): Can an LLM immediately produce high-quality output?
Dimension 5 - Hallucination Reduction (0-10): Does the prompt minimize fabrication risk?
Overall score = weighted average. Production threshold: 70/100 minimum, 80/100 recommended.
Scoring below 60 indicates fundamental structural issues requiring rework, not refinement.

TITLE: RAG-Enhanced Prompt Generation
CATEGORY: advanced
---
Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) improves prompt quality by grounding generation in proven knowledge.
RAG retrieval strategy: embed the user requirement, retrieve top-K relevant knowledge chunks.
Retrieved knowledge should cover: domain best practices, proven patterns, anti-patterns to avoid.
Inject retrieved context at prompt start: "Using these proven best practices: [context], generate..."
RAG improves specificity by referencing concrete techniques rather than generic advice.
Keep retrieved chunks focused: 3-5 short, highly relevant chunks outperform many long ones.
After generation, verify the output incorporates the retrieved knowledge — not just paraphrases it.
